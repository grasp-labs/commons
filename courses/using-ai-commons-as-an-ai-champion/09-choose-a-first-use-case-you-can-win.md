## Choose a first use case you can win

A good first candidate is **frequent** (people feel the relief), **low-stakes** (a mistake is survivable), **unambiguous** (everyone agrees what correct looks like), and **owned by someone who actually wants it**.

**What this looks like in practice:**

> ✅ **Good first use case** — "Draft the weekly team-update email from our project notes." It happens *every week* (frequent), a wrong draft is edited before it's sent (low-stakes), the format is well understood (unambiguous), and one person already owns the update (owned).
>
> ❌ **Bad first use case** — "Reconcile the quarterly finance numbers." Rare (once a quarter), high-stakes if wrong, the rules have edge cases (ambiguous), and ownership is split across teams. Win somewhere else first.

The metric that matters isn't accuracy on day one — it's **repeat use** by week two.

**Record the baseline before you start** — how long the task takes today, how often it has to be redone. Without a baseline you will end up arguing about impressions.

> **The metric that matters is repeat use.** Anyone will try something once. Coming back next week is the only real signal.
