**Your first two weeks — a checklist**

- [ ] Pick one colleague and one specific annoyance to remove *(Step 2)*
- [ ] **Search before you build** — check it doesn't already exist *(Step 5)*
- [ ] Choose a use case you can win: frequent, low-stakes, unambiguous, owned *(Step 9)*
- [ ] Capture what you learn as a **playbook** so the system keeps it *(Step 7)*
- [ ] Have one **trust conversation** with a nervous stakeholder *(Step 8)*
- [ ] Teach one other person to do it without you *(Step 10)*

**Four ways Champions fail:** the hero · the graveyard playbook · boiling the ocean · tool tourism.

You'll know it worked not when *you* use it — but when a colleague you taught uses it without asking you.

### Four ways Champions fail

- **The hero** — everything routes through you, and adoption dies the week you take leave.
- **The graveyard playbook** — written once, never revised, quietly wrong.
- **Boiling the ocean** — ten pilots at ten per cent each finish nothing.
- **Tool tourism** — enthusiasm for the platform rather than the work. Nobody adopts a tool; they adopt a solved problem.

> **The last checkbox is the one that matters.** The rest proves you can do it. That one proves it outlives you.
